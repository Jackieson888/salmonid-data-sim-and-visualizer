# Field Notes: Steelhead Trout
### *Oncorhynchus mykiss* (Walbaum, 1792)
*Compiled from USFWS "Freshwater Fish of America" series, Sept. 2023*

---

## Quick ID

- **What it actually is:** a migratory form of rainbow trout — same species, different life strategy
- **Look:** generally more silvery and larger-bodied than resident rainbow trout, but appearance alone isn't reliable
- **The only certain ID methods:** scale analysis, or checking the chemical signature locked into the ear bones (otoliths)
- **Typical length:** 18–24 in, with reports up to **45 in**

## The Rainbow Trout Connection — A Genetic Coin Flip

This is the strangest thing about steelhead: the migratory/resident split isn't fixed by lineage.

- Two steelhead parents can produce offspring that stay in fresh water forever, becoming ordinary rainbow trout
- Two resident rainbow trout can produce offspring that go migratory and become steelhead

The same gene pool, expressing itself two different ways depending on... something still not fully mapped out.

## Life Strategy — Built to Repeat

Unlike Pacific salmon, steelhead are **iteroparous** — they don't die after spawning. A single fish can return to spawn in its home stream **multiple times across its life**, sometimes in consecutive years, sometimes skipping a year in between.

```
hatch → juvenile in freshwater (months to ~1 year, variable)
  → smolt → ocean/estuary (months to several years, variable)
  → return to natal stream to spawn
  → survive → return to ocean → repeat spawning trip (unlike Pacific salmon)
```

Both ends of this cycle are loosely timed — some juveniles bolt for salt water within months of hatching, others linger up to a year; some adults spend only months at sea before returning, others stay for years. Researchers still don't have migration timing fully worked out.

## Range & Habitat

- **Native range:** North America, west of the Rocky Mountains
- **Habitat shifts by life stage:** freshwater rivers/streams and lakes for spawning → brackish estuary as a transit zone → open ocean for the adult non-spawning phase
- Most of a steelhead's year is actually spent at sea or in estuaries — freshwater is a spawning-only stopover, not a home base

## Diet — Scales Up With Age

| Life stage | Diet |
|---|---|
| Young | zooplankton |
| Mature | fish eggs, crustaceans, mollusks, small fish, and — occasionally — **mice** |

## Field Sketch Notes

*Two fish, same species, on the same page: a slimmer, more heavily-spotted resident rainbow holding in a shaded pool, next to a broader, chrome-silver steelhead fresh from the ocean — same otolith chemistry story written on two very different bodies.*

## Conservation Status

Steelhead are notably sensitive to human disturbance — habitat degradation, fishing pressure, disease, and a tendency to hybridize with other trout (including non-native introductions) all weigh on native populations.

- **12 Distinct Population Segments (DPS)** recognized along the U.S. West Coast by NOAA/NMFS, currently including:
  - 1 endangered DPS
  - 10 threatened DPS
  - 2 experimental populations
  - 1 species-of-concern segment
- The National Fish Hatchery System raises **over 6 million steelhead annually** — supporting recreational and commercial fishing, tribal/subsistence harvest, and recovery of threatened populations
- Steelhead rank among the **top five recreational fish species in North America** and hold significant cultural importance to Native American tribes
